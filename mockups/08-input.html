Action input

Today you pick a move on a D-pad in the right column and read why it is illegal from a tooltip. This puts the choice back on the board: five translucent ghost bodies show where your index 18 would land and at which world turn, a radial menu gives inversion the same weight as a step, and one commit bar carries the sealed state, who is still choosing, and any predicted contest.

  • 10Each petal repeats the ghost's two numbers, so the menu and the board agree without the eye moving.

  • 12Nothing is blocked here, so the blocked petal is described rather than drawn. It would be struck through, unclickable, and carry the same reason text as the board chip.

Commit bar

One bar under the board replaces the commit button, the still-choosing line, the priority card, and the change-my-action button. Three states, in order.

  • 13Sealed envelope, not a greyed button. Your action is hidden from the others until everyone commits, and the envelope is the only icon that says "written down and closed".
  • 14Still choosing lists the people you are waiting on as avatars plus names. Committed players dim rather than disappear, so the count never jumps.
  • 15Change my action is a plain button next to the seal, with Esc written on it. Today it is a separate card that only appears after you commit.
  • 16The contest warning names the tile, the other mover, the priority order, and the outcome in that order. Priority is the real one for this turn: Mira, then Rook, then Ash, then Juno.
Honest note on state 3. No contest is possible on this turn of the fixture. Rook walks backward into t7 while Juno and Ash walk forward into t8 and Mira into t1, so no two players create a body at the same world turn. State 3 is drawn with Juno moved to a backward playhead so the component has something to say. The priority order and the resulting sentence are the real ones.

Keyboard flow

Trade-offs

Pros

  • The index and the world turn an action produces stop being something you compute. They are printed on the tile you are about to take.
  • Illegal moves carry their reason where you are looking, which is the design doc's "illegal moves and their reason on inspection" without requiring a hover.
  • Inversion stops looking like a fifth movement button. Both the badge and the ring say it costs an index and no world turn.
  • One bar collapses four separate pieces of chrome and gives the contest rule a place to explain itself in words.

Cons

  • Five ghosts plus trails plus the key mark is a lot on one tile at small cell sizes. The full-board figure works at 76px cells; at 44px the two numbers stop fitting.
  • Ghosts sit where other players' history sits, so a ghost can cover a body you wanted to read before choosing.
  • A radial menu needs a press-and-hold, which competes with the long-press that page 11 wants for inspecting a body.
  • A predicted contest is a guess about an opponent's hidden action. Showing it may teach the priority rule or may just be noise.

What it answers

It answers illegal moves and their reason on inspection directly, and it answers part of player identity and personal index on every body by extending index labelling from real bodies to the ones you are about to make. It gives the priority rule, which today is a card reading "Holders resolve before movers", a concrete sentence about a specific tile.

It does not answer how shooting and placed objects should work in the controls, but it opens a slot: the radial has four free outer segments and the ghost layer already distinguishes "a tile you take" from "a tile you affect". It also does not answer how touch users inspect a history body, and it makes that harder by claiming long-press for the menu.

Cost

S to M. The ghost layer and the commit bar are ui.tsx and style.css only. view.actions already carries action, reason, t, x, y, and move for all six actions, which is everything a ghost needs; the Cell component already receives the matching ActionOffer and only has to draw more with it. The radial menu is the larger piece, mostly pointer handling and an SVG arc helper.

Two things need the view to say more. Reason strings today are wall, occupied, off the board, and that is before the start of time; turning "occupied" into "Juno's live body is here" needs _blockReason to return the occupying colour alongside the reason, which is a view change, not a rules change. The contest warning needs the set of opponents whose legal targets include your target tile, which the engine can derive from the same occupancy map but does not expose. Both are small additions to View.
