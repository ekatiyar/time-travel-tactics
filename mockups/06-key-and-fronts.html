Key & front language

Today the key is a small yellow square on one edge of a body, a front is a small triangle, and the number of turns until that front reaches you lives only in hover text. This page gives the key, the side it came from, the tile it can be stolen from, and the front that is coming for it one shared language, and puts the countdown on the board.

Key custody ladder

The key has one timeline of its own. Index 0 to 5 read "on the center tile at that world turn". After a grab, each later index reads the grabber's next body. Step through the meta-turns to watch Juno's front eat yours.

Inline readout

Why a countdown and not a triangle

The gap is the one number a bootstrap player has to act on. It says how many actions are left to spend the key: reach t0 beside your spawn, or hand the race to somebody else. Today that number is hover-only, so it is invisible on a phone and invisible to anyone scanning the board.

The wedge shape carries direction, the badge carries time, the ribbon carries what is at stake. Reading any one of them alone still tells you something true, which is the test a symbol set has to pass.

It extends to weapons

Bootstrap fronts only erase custody. The planned weapons mode adds fronts that erase bodies, and a second front that restores them. Same wedge, same two-index rate, same badge. Fill tells the two apart, and the span between them is drawn as a hole rather than as two unrelated markers.

Variant: the erasure and restoration pair

Trade-offs

Pros

  • The gap number lands on the board, where it is readable on touch and at a glance.
  • The steal tile stops being a rule you have to remember and becomes a square you can see.
  • One wedge with three fills covers bootstrap fronts and both planned weapon fronts, so the alphabet does not grow when weapons land.
  • The ladder shows a fact the board structurally cannot: the key revisits tiles, so custody is only legible on its own axis.
  • Nothing here needs new engine state. Keys, fronts, gap and key sides are already in the session view.

Cons

  • The ribbon crosses world turns on a board whose other marks are all at the focus turn. That is a new kind of line and it can be misread as a walking route.
  • Four glowing marks plus a ring plus chevrons is a lot of ink for one key. On a 5x5 board it would crowd badly.
  • The ladder is a second chart to learn, and it needs horizontal room that portrait phones do not have.
  • A steal ring is drawn per key-holding body. With two holders and several recorded bodies that is many rings.
  • The glow and chain are decorative weight on a prototype whose current marks are deliberately flat.

What it answers

Answers the UI requirement to show the personal-index gap for an inbound front, and sketches the requirement for erasure and restoration fronts with the hole between them. Makes a first proposal on the open question of how fronts should read on the board and the strip: a wedge that faces its direction of travel, with the gap as a badge.

Leaves open the question of whether an opponent front readout should count to their last visible body or to their present, because the ladder still shows what you can see, not what exists. It also does not settle how many rings and ribbons a small board can carry; the threat layer on page 10 proposes an intensity control for exactly that.

Cost

Board marks: S. Key token, notch, chain, steal ring, wedge and badge are all ui.tsx cell decorations plus style.css. The steal tile needs the tile beyond a holder's key side, which is one vector add from data already in view.keys.

Ribbon: M. Drawing the key's timeline across world turns means the board has to draw marks that do not belong to the focus turn. That is a real change to how the board composes, not a new mark.

Ladder: M. A new component. It needs the key timeline as indices with owners and front positions. ModeView currently exposes holders and front positions but not the index each holder sits at, so this needs a small view addition in bootstrap.ts: the index alongside each key entry, and each front's origin.

Erasure and restoration: L, and blocked on weapons existing at all.
