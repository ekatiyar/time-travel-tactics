Art direction

The prototype is deliberately neutral: two dark greys, one light grey, four hues and a yellow, all flat. That is a placeholder, not a decision. This page draws the same six-by-five crop of the fixture five times, once in the current tokens and once in each of four directions, and judges each on the three things this game is hard to read at a glance: which way a body is walking, what its personal index is, and where the key and the fronts are.

Recommendation

Keep the flat neutral base and buy two things. None of the four is worth adopting whole. Three of them fail the board size the game is heading towards, and two of them are a second full palette before they render a pixel. But the current look is not finished either: it spends colour on identity and nothing on urgency, which is why the key and the fronts are the weakest reads today.

Buy the annotation grammar from Blueprint. Mono labels, leader lines and a dimension line with a number on it. That is exactly the shape of the facts this game has to state: gap 4, index 14 to 17, t11. It costs one type style and a line style, it works in both themes, and it is the only one of the four that makes the rules easier to say out loud.

Buy the bloom from Neon, for two objects only. The key and the fronts. Additive glow is the one perceptual channel the current design has not spent, it is hue-independent so it does not fight the four player colours, and it degrades to a plain bright fill when the radius is capped on small cells. Nothing else on the board is allowed to glow, which is what makes it mean urgency.

Reject Pixel and Tabletop as the whole direction, and keep both in reserve. Pixel is the best of the four at the size the prototype runs at now and the worst at the size it is heading to; if boards stay at 16 columns it is a real candidate and the cheapest of the four. Tabletop is the best answer to direction anyone here has, the flipped token, and that idea can be lifted into the flat style without the wood. It is also the right look for a replay or share image, where the board is large and still.

Trade-offs

Pros

  • Judging four directions on one crop of real fixture data makes the comparison about legibility rather than taste. The crowded tile (7,4) breaks three of the five styles and that shows up immediately.
  • The recommendation is two additions, an annotation grammar and a bloom reserved for two objects, rather than a re-skin, so it can ship in pieces.
  • Bloom for the key and the fronts uses a channel the four player hues do not use, which is the open question about urgency answered with something other than a fifth colour.
  • The token table says exactly which directions need new tokens rather than new values, which is the real cost line.

Cons

  • Five hand-drawn panels are five renderers. Nothing here is proof that any of them survives contact with a full 16×9 board in motion, with stacks, bounces and inversions.
  • The 20px row is a still image of a size problem. The honest test is a live board at that size, which this page does not give.
  • Only dark styles were pushed hard. Tabletop is judged partly on being light, which is a fair cost but not a fair aesthetic comparison.
  • Additive glow has an accessibility cost that is not measured here: it lowers text contrast near the glowing object and it is exactly the effect that some players turn down.
  • The flipped-token idea for direction is recommended without being drawn in the flat style, so it is a claim, not a mockup.

What it answers

It gives what visual treatment distinguishes urgent threats without overloading player colours a concrete answer with a drawing behind it: additive bloom, reserved for the key and for fronts, hue-independent, capped in radius on small cells.

It serves identity and personal index on every body by measuring each direction against the numeral, and it is where the page is most useful as a negative result: three of the four candidate styles make the index harder to read than the placeholder does.

It leaves how erasure and restoration fronts read untouched, because none of these styles was drawn with a hole in the timeline in it. It also leaves the animation language to page 07; a style that looks good still can still fail in motion.

Cost

S for the recommendation, L for any of the four whole. The annotation grammar is a type style, a 1px leader line and a label component in ui.tsx, plus two tokens in style.css for the line and the label ink. The bloom is one SVG filter and a rule that only the key square and front triangles may reference it, plus a radius that is a function of cell size, which is a few lines in the board renderer.

Adopting a direction whole is a different job. Each needs the ten tokens above re-picked in both themes, and three of them need new tokens the theme system does not have: a hatch pattern and a leader colour for blueprint, a checker pair and a unit size for pixel, a bloom radius and a streak taper for neon, a paper texture and a shadow model for tabletop. Pixel additionally needs the board renderer to snap to a unit grid, which means giving up the current cqmin scaling in style.css; that is the only one of the four that changes how the board is laid out rather than how it is painted. None of them needs an engine or view change.
