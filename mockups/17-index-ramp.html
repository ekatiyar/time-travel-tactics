Index ramp & body glyphs

Two open questions meet on one chip. Is a personal-index colour ramp needed to read target value at a glance, and what marks an urgent threat without stealing a channel the four player colours already use? This page builds the glyph alphabet first, then tries three ramps on the fixture board, then proposes a chip that answers both without a ramp.

Glyph alphabet at three cell sizes

44px is a comfortable desktop cell on a 16×9 board. 28px is what a 16×9 board gets in a 480px phone column. 18px is a 24×16 board on the same phone. Every glyph below is the same drawing scaled; nothing is redrawn for small sizes, which is the honest test.

The proposal

Recommendation

Do not add a personal-index colour ramp. It fails on three counts. The lightness axis is already spent: age within the look-back window uses opacity, and stacking a second lightness signal on top means one body can be dark because it is old in world time or dark because it is low in personal index. The four hues do not have equal room, Teal and Purple lose a ramp that Coral and Amber keep, so the same index reads differently per player. And the range is wrong: with a cap of 43 a ramp must resolve 44 steps, where the eye gets about five reliably, so a ramp would say "low, middle, high" and the number already says 14.

Index is a numeral, shown where it matters. On the focus layer and on the live body always; on recorded bodies on hover and tap. That keeps the board quiet at 28px and keeps the answer exact when asked. It also matches how the number is used: you compare two specific bodies, you do not scan for the highest.

Urgency gets a neutral channel, never a hue. A warn-coloured edge bar plus the gap numeral, on the body a front will hit. Because it is the only warn-coloured thing on the board, it survives to 18px as a bar even when its numeral does not, and it does not compete with the four player colours or with the key's yellow, which sits inside the chip rather than on its edge.

If target value does turn out to need a glance-level signal once weapons exist, the honest lever is size, not colour: a body worth more downstream bodies gets a larger pill. Size survives 18px, is hue-independent, and cannot be confused with age.

Trade-offs

Pros

  • Nothing is added to the colour system, so the four hues keep their whole range for identity.
  • The board gets quieter. Today every history dot and every focus chip carries ink; here only the focus layer and the live bodies do.
  • The live body becomes findable in one look, which is the most common question at the start of a turn.
  • Threat has its own colour and its own shape, so "am I about to lose the key" is answered without reading a panel.
  • Everything degrades predictably: at 18px you lose numerals and keep identity, direction, key and threat.

Cons

  • Hover is not a touch gesture. Tap-to-reveal needs a real tap target, and a recorded chip at 28px is below the 44px minimum.
  • Hiding numbers on recorded bodies makes comparing two distant bodies a two-step job instead of a glance.
  • The pulse is motion on a board that is otherwise still, which is a cost for motion-sensitive players. It needs a reduced-motion fallback, here a static double ring.
  • A warn-coloured edge bar is a fifth colour on the board. It is neutral against the four player hues but not against the key's yellow, and the two sit close on Amber chips.
  • It leaves the ramp question technically unanswered rather than settled: it argues the question is the wrong one, which is only convincing once weapons exist to test it.

What it answers

It closes is a personal-index colour ramp needed for target value at a glance with a no, and shows the work: three ramps drawn on the real board, at the generous normalisation, with the specific hue that fails in each. It gives a concrete answer to what visual treatment distinguishes urgent threats without overloading player colours: one neutral warn channel carrying an edge bar and a numeral, reserved for fronts.

It partly serves identity and personal index on every body, and is honest that it trades glanceability of the index for a quieter board. It does not address how touch users inspect a history body, which the hover-to-reveal rule makes more urgent, not less. Page 03's ribbons and page 09's inspector are the places that has to be solved.

Cost

S. This is the smallest change on the board of any direction here, and it is all in ui.tsx and style.css. Drop the label on bodies whose t is not the focus turn, add a :hover and :focus-visible rule to bring it back, add a second ring element on bodies with live, and add a keyframe pulse behind a prefers-reduced-motion guard. The glyph alphabet above is documentation, not code.

The urgency bar needs one thing the view does not hand over cleanly today. view.fronts gives a front's world turn, tile and gap, but the bar belongs on a specific body, so the renderer has to match the front's (t,x,y) back to a body of the target colour. That is a lookup the board already does for keys, so it is a few lines, not an engine change. Ruling out the ramp is what saves the real cost: no palette work, no per-hue tuning, no colour-blind pass on 44 steps.
