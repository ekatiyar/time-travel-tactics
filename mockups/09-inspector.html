Body inspector

Today a body is a hover title: one sentence, gone on mouse-out, unreachable on a touch screen. This direction makes a body a thing you open. One tap gives its index, its clock, its key, the front coming for it, and its whole personal timeline, and the same panel explains a tile you cannot move into.

  • 5Eighteen beads, one per personal index, with the world turn under each. Juno's two inversions and the two key indices are visible without reading the log.
  • 6Three lines only: who and index, clock and direction, then the one fact that matters here, the key and its side.
  • 7The press ring answers the open question about touch. Hover cannot exist on a phone, so the same card is on a 600 ms hold, and the panel opens on release instead of replacing the board.
  • 8Pinned bodies keep their panel while you inspect elsewhere, so a comparison survives the next board click.
  • 9Rows are the same fields in the same order, so the difference is the only thing that moves: both hold a key, one of the two keys stops being real in 4 turns.
  • 10A wall reason is one word plus the rule. Today this text exists only as a title attribute that reads "Blocked by wall", which a touch user can never see.
  • 11A body reason names the body, the tile, and the world turn, so you can inspect that body next and learn when it leaves.

Trade-offs

Pros

  • Answers two UI requirements with one surface: identity plus index on a body, and the reason a move is illegal.
  • Works on touch, where the current title tooltip is simply unreachable.
  • The bead row shows inversions, key custody, and the front against one player at a glance. Nothing else in the UI does.
  • Pin turns "who wins the key race" from mental arithmetic into two columns.

Cons

  • A 352 px panel steals board width on laptops; on a phone it has to become a bottom sheet, which is page 11's problem.
  • Click-to-inspect competes with click-to-move on the board. Needs a rule: inspect only outside your own turn, or only on a second tap.
  • Eighteen beads fit. Forty-three, the turn cap, do not. The row needs to collapse or scroll late in a match.
  • More surfaces telling the same story means more places for the front number to go stale.

What it answers

Closes illegal moves and their reason on inspection: the reason string the engine already produces gets a place to live that is not a tooltip. Closes identity and personal index on every body for stacked tiles, where the board can only print "16 … 18". Shows the personal-index gap for an inbound front as a sentence with the number in it. Proposes an answer to how should touch users inspect a history body: long press for the card, release for the panel, pin for comparison.

Leaves open: erasure and restoration fronts, which will need a second front row and a hole marker on the bead strip; and the cycle countdown, which has no representation here yet.

Cost

M. The panel, the hover card, and the bead row are ui.tsx plus style.css only: every field shown comes from SessionView today, including actions[].reason, keys[].side, and fronts[].gap. Pin mode is a second selection in component state. The one engine-view change worth asking for is a link from a front to the body index it currently sits on, so the panel can say "on your body 14" without the UI searching bodies by position.
