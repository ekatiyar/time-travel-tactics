Turn resolution animation

Today a resolved turn simply re-renders. Every rule that confuses new players (simultaneous moves, bounces, inversion, grabs, fronts advancing two per turn) is an event that could be shown as motion over about a second. These loops show one proposal per event. Nothing here needs new data: the log already records every event kind.

Timing and ordering

A resolved turn plays as one 1.2s sequence. Players who commit fast should not wait on it, so any input during playback skips to the end.

OffsetEventDurationSkippable
0.00sReveal: lids open, chosen action chips appear on the turn card0.25syes
0.25sMoves: all bodies slide at once, blocked movers spring back, hold rings pulse0.40syes
0.65sInversions: squash and refill, arc, playhead flip (only for players who inverted)0.30syes
0.65sGrabs: key slides to the holder's side, flash, custody band grows0.30syes
0.95sFronts: every wedge steps two beads; passed keys grey out; ETAs tick0.25sno, it is the most important cue
1.20sTransition: old bodies settle into dots, focus label ticks, controls unlock0.20syes

With prefers-reduced-motion the sequence collapses to a 0.15s crossfade and the event log gains a one-line summary per event instead.

Trade-offs

Pros

  • Turns rules into things players see happen. The bounce and the 2:1 front step are the two ideas the docs say are hardest to read, and both become visible.
  • Cheap: CSS transitions on existing DOM tokens for moves, a few keyframes for the rest. No canvas.
  • Works with every layout on other pages; it is orthogonal to board style.

Cons

  • The current Board re-renders cells from scratch; animating requires stable keys per body so tokens move rather than re-mount. That is a real refactor of Board and Cell.
  • Scrubbing history while an animation plays needs a rule (cancel animation, snap).
  • Four players and a 24x13 board can produce many simultaneous events; keep the total under 1.5s and let players skip.

What it answers

Directly addresses the open question on whether the front readout is clear: the countdown becomes a moving thing with an ETA that visibly ticks. It also gives blocked moves a non-verbal explanation to pair with the reason text.

Cost

M. Requires keyed, persistent token elements in ui.tsx and a small "last turn events" diff (the View already has events with kinds moved, held, inverted, blocked, stuck, grab, lost). No engine changes.
