Board-first layout

The board is the game, so it gets the window. Everything else becomes a thin edge: a status HUD on top, a timeline dock on the bottom, and two rails that collapse to icon strips. Today's three-card grid gives the 16x9 board about a third of a 1440-wide window; this gives it two thirds, and more than that once the rails fold.

Trade-offs

Pros

  • The board roughly doubles in area at 1440 wide, and bodies stop competing with the index numbers drawn on them.
  • Status that never changes shape stays in a fixed 46px band, so the eye learns one place to look for index, turn, and direction.
  • The timeline controls stop being two unlabelled sliders in the middle of the page and become one dock you can point at.
  • Rails collapse, so the layout degrades to a tablet or a narrow window by folding rather than reflowing into a column stack.

Cons

  • Full-height chrome means the page no longer scrolls; a short window squeezes the dock and the rails rather than letting the page grow.
  • Collapsed rails hide the log, and the three toasts cannot carry a whole turn of four players' events.
  • A hover-only legend is unreachable on touch and needs a tap target as well.
  • Overlay toasts and the focus-mode scrim both draw over the board, which is the thing the layout is meant to protect.

What it answers

It addresses one combined board for present and history by giving that board the space to hold trails, stacks, and key marks at 57 to 74px cells instead of 44. It addresses a timeline strip with each playhead and direction twice: compactly in the dock, and per player in focus mode. It addresses the personal-index gap for an inbound front by promoting Juno's front from a hover tooltip to a HUD chip that reads "front reaches you in 4".

It leaves open the erasure and restoration fronts question, since bootstrap has one front kind and no holes to draw. It does not answer how touch users inspect a history body; hover popovers and a hover legend make that worse, and page 11 handles it. It does not decide what visual treatment marks urgency, it only reserves a slot for it in the HUD.

Cost

M. The board, strip, log, and legend components already exist and are passed a view; this is mostly a new PlayScreen shell plus CSS. Changes land in ui.tsx and style.css only: replace the .cols grid with a three-row viewport grid, add two rail-collapse booleans in component state, move the two input[type=range] rows into the dock, and wrap Legend in a popover.

Two pieces need more than layout work. Per-player lanes in focus mode need a per-colour playhead history, which the view does not expose today: it has bodies with t and dir, so lanes can be derived in the UI, but doing it per frame over a long match wants a memo. The key band needs the key's index-to-holder reading, which the mode view already produces for keys and keyAtCenter. Neither is an engine rules change.
