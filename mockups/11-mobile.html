Mobile portrait

On a 390px phone the whole prototype is one scrolling column: the board sits mid-page at about 320px wide and the D-pad is two screens below it, so choosing a move means scrolling away from the thing you are choosing on. This direction fixes the board to the top at full width, puts every control in the thumb zone, and moves the two things that need space, the log and the per-player timeline, into a drawer. Inspection becomes a long press that opens a bottom sheet, which is the open question this page exists to answer.

What 22px cells do to the index labels

Focus t7 with three turns of look-back, so two stacks are in view: Juno 7 and 8 share (4,4) walking opposite ways, and Ash 14 and 17 share (14,0). Each crop is the full 16×9 board at exactly the size a phone gives it.

A · today's rule at 22px

Label size is 0.26 of the cell, so 5.7px. A two-digit index is 3px tall at the x-height. The key square is 3.5px, a trail dot is 2.6px across and the front triangle is 3.1px wide.

B · labels forced to 9px, the smallest legible mono

A single two-digit index just fits the 15px pill. "7·8" and "14·17" are 22px and 35px wide and spill past the 19px stacked pill, into neighbouring tiles.

C · proposed: numerals only where they fit

The live body carries a floating badge outside the cell. Recorded bodies keep hue, fill and key square and give up their number to the sheet. Trail dots go to a 2px floor, key squares to 5px, fronts to 10px.

Reach, and what happens on a bigger board

Trade-offs

Pros

  • The board and the controls are on screen together. Today they are 900px apart on a 390px phone, which is the single biggest mobile problem.
  • Nothing is cut. The log, the per-player timeline and the world-turn and look-back controls all live in the drawer at full width, where they are easier to read than in the desktop right column.
  • Long press plus a bottom sheet is the standard touch idiom for "tell me about this", so it needs no teaching, and the sheet has room for the front explanation that will not fit in a tooltip.
  • The sheet is a real answer to inspection on touch and it also works with a mouse, so desktop and mobile can share one inspector rather than two.
  • Tap targets are honest: 96×56 buttons and a 60px commit, all above the 44px minimum.

Cons

  • Swipe down on your body to invert collides with swipe down on the D-pad to move south, and with the system pull-to-refresh gesture in a browser. It needs the swipe to start on the body itself and a visible confirm step.
  • The sheet covers the D-pad, so inspecting during your turn hides your controls. It has to be dismissible with one thumb and must not clear a pending choice.
  • Personal index on every body, a stated UI requirement, is not met at 22px. Only the live body keeps a numeral; the rest need a press.
  • A 22px cell is half the 44px touch minimum, so the press target has to be padded and two bodies on neighbouring tiles will be mis-hit. On a stacked tile the sheet must offer both bodies.
  • Three surfaces can be open at once, sheet over drawer over board, and the dismiss order has to be defined.
  • Landscape is not solved here. In landscape the board wants the left two thirds and the thumb zone moves to both edges, which is a different layout, not this one rotated.

What it answers

It answers how should touch users inspect a history body: long press for 400ms, bottom sheet, six fields, dismiss by dragging down. The sheet is the only place a recorded body's personal index appears on a phone, which makes inspection load-bearing rather than optional, so the press target and the stacked-tile case have to be built properly.

It keeps a timeline strip with each playhead and direction at full width in the collapsed state and gives each player a row in the drawer, where the key band and the front are visible together.

It leaves identity and personal index on every body unmet at phone size, and says so with numbers rather than hiding it. It does not address erasure and restoration fronts, which have no mobile-specific answer yet, and it does not solve boards larger than about 18 columns without a zoomed viewport, which is a separate direction.

Cost

M. The layout is ui.tsx and style.css only. Today's @media(max-width:1120px) rule already collapses the three columns into one and reorders the board first; this replaces that with a fixed grid of HUD, board, strip, drawer and pad, plus position:sticky on the pad. The HUD is a recomposition of strings the view already provides.

The sheet reuses whatever the inspector on page 09 becomes; the mobile-specific work is the long-press detector, a pointer handler with a 400ms timer and a 10px movement cancel, and hit padding on bodies. The drawer is a transform on a fixed panel with two snap points.

Two things need the engine view rather than CSS. The per-body front answer, "this body is hit in 4 turns", needs the front matched back to a body by colour and index, the same lookup page 17 needs. The minimum sizes, a 2px trail dot floor and a 5px key square, mean the board renderer needs a floor per mark instead of pure cqmin scaling, which is a change to the cell CSS in style.css and nothing else. A pinch-zoomed viewport for boards past 18 columns is the one item here that is genuinely L, and it is not needed for 16×9.
