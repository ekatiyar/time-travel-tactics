Replay & post-match review

Today the match ends with one line of text and the advice to drag the world-turn slider. A match is a list of actions the engine can replay exactly, so the ending should be a readable document: what happened, when the key changed hands, and a picture worth sending to the other player.

Honesty note. Meta-turns 0 to 17 are engine data from the fixture. Meta-turns 18 to 33 are a hand-checked continuation, because the fixture stops at 17 and no player can win before then. The continuation is rule-legal and checked against the board: Rook cannot re-grab while it still holds a key, so it parks on (8,3) at t7 with its key side against the wall at (9,3), which makes it un-stealable; Juno’s front strips Rook on meta-turn 21; on 22 Rook takes the key back from Juno’s recorded body p17 at the same world turn, starting a front at key index 7 that Juno’s older front can never reach, because fronts only move forward. The scrub is interactive over 0 to 17 and marks the boundary; the draw-at-cap banner is shown as a variant below.

Variants

Trade-offs

Pros

  • Chapters come from view.events, which already carries a meta-turn, a kind, a colour and a tile. No new engine data.
  • The custody rows answer a rules question the design doc raises: whether two indices per meta-turn is the right front rate. You can see the gap close.
  • One scrub component serves post-match review and in-match rewind.
  • A share card built from the board renderer cannot drift out of date the way a hand-drawn one would.

Cons

  • Needs a view at an earlier meta-turn, which the engine does not expose today.
  • Fourteen chapters on a 43-turn bar is dense. Only six are labelled; the rest need the list beside them.
  • Rewind during a live match is a way to miss your turn. The warning rail helps, it does not fix it.
  • The share card leaks the result to anyone with the link, including a player who has not opened the match yet.

What it answers

It covers the UI requirement for a timeline strip with playheads and direction, at the scale of a whole match rather than one turn, and it puts the personal-index gap of an inbound front on screen as a distance you watch close. It gives the front-rate open question an instrument: the custody rows show Juno’s front taking six meta-turns to cross from its grab to Rook’s present, and Rook’s taking five to cross back.

It leaves open whether replay should respect the horizon. Today view() clips bodies and events to the viewer’s horizon; after the match that clipping hides the thing you are reviewing. It also leaves open how a spectator, who has no colour, enters the replay at all.

What the engine already gives you, and what it does not

Already thereWould need building
Match stores config plus an action log of {turn, color, action}, and _derive() replays the whole log from turn 0 on every change. Replay is the normal path, not a new one. A view at an earlier meta-turn. view(color) only derives the head state and the cache holds one derived state, so a scrub needs either a prefix argument to _derive or a second Match built from a log prefix.
view.events already carries the meta-turn, so chapter marks need no new field. Grabs, inversions, blocks and losses are all there. A decision on the horizon after the match ends. Review wants every body; view() filters to me.horizon. That is one flag, and one rule about who may set it.
Keys and fronts at an earlier turn come free from bootstrap.view(s, turn, horizon) once the state is re-derived. Nothing changes in the mode. Custody as a series. Today you can read who holds a key now; a chart over 43 turns means recording one row per meta-turn during the replay, which is a loop around the existing view call.
export() already encodes config, log and names into the string behind the resume link. A replay link is that string. Card rendering. The board is SVG, so a card is a serialise-and-rasterise step, plus somewhere to host the image for a link preview to fetch.

Cost of replay itself is not a concern. Forty-three turns of four players is a few hundred tile resolutions; deriving a fresh state for every 600 ms frame is far cheaper than the render.

Cost

Rewind and the scrub: M. The chapter bar, custody chart, transport and event panel are ui.tsx and style.css only, but they all depend on the one engine change: a view at an earlier meta-turn. That change is small and sits inside core.ts, where _derive already loops turn by turn.

Post-match screen: S once rewind exists, since it is the same components on a finished match.

Share card: M to L, and mostly not UI work. Rendering is easy; serving an image at a stable URL for a link preview needs hosting the prototype does not have.
