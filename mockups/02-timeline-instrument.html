Timeline instrument

Promote the strip from a row of 5px dots to the second half of the game view: one row per player, each player's zig-zag through world time laid out as legs, the key's own timeline as a custody band, and fronts shown where they will land. The board answers "where"; this answers "when".

  • 4Beads are bodies. Filled means walking in your direction, hollow means opposing, matching the board. Every fourth bead carries its personal index. Hovering shows all.

Variants

Interaction

  • Scrub by dragging the focus handle or clicking a column. The board re-renders at that world turn. The row for your colour stays highlighted so you never lose your own line.
  • Hover a bead to highlight the same body on the board and show a compact inspector (page 09).
  • Hover a front to draw its projected path on both the strip and the board, with the meta-turn at which it lands.
  • Click a custody segment to focus the world turn where that grab happened.
  • Keyboard: [ and ] step the focus turn, Home returns to your present.

Trade-offs

Pros

  • Every UI requirement about time is satisfied in one place: playheads, direction, fronts, gaps, and the eventual holes.
  • Inversion legs make "I can overlap my own past" visible before the player tries it.
  • The custody band explains bootstrap's odd rule that an earlier grab beats a later one, which the current triangle does not.
  • Scales with the cap: 43 turns at 1400px is 26px per turn; 400 turns needs zoom, which the fog already implies.

Cons

  • Vertical cost: four rows plus the band is about 260px. On laptops the board shrinks unless the layout is board-first with a collapsible dock.
  • Lanes per leg grow with repeated inversions. Teal inverted three times at t9 and needs four lanes in one column. Cap lanes at 4 and collapse the rest into a stack glyph.
  • Two axes (world turn vs personal index) are both useful. Offering both risks confusion; pick world turn as default and expose the other as a toggle for the front readout.

What it answers

Directly answers the open question about the front readout: the wedge sits on the body it will hit, and the ETA badge counts meta-turns, not indices. It answers "how should fronts read on the strip" for bootstrap and sketches the erasure/restoration hole as a shaded span between two wedges for the weapons mode. It leaves open whether an opponent's horizon should show; here rows only draw what your horizon reveals.

Cost

M. Pure view work in ui.tsx and style.css. Everything drawn here comes from the existing View: bodies with p, t, dir, live; keys by index; fronts with t, target, gap; keyAtCenter. The custody band needs the key's holder per index, which the mode view does not expose yet. That is a small addition to the bootstrap view. Drag scrubbing replaces the two range inputs.
