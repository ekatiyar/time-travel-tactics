Setup & lobby

Today setup is seven form fields and a colour list: you pick numbers, press Create match, and only then find out what the board looks like. This direction shows the board while you choose it, turns the mode select into two cards that state the win condition, and moves seating onto the board so your colour and your corner are the same decision.

Trade-offs

Pros

  • You see the board before committing to it. Wall generation is seeded, so a preview is exact, not indicative.
  • Colour and corner become one choice, which is how players think about a board anyway.
  • Mode cards state the win condition. The dropdown label could not.
  • Three share routes cover chat, a phone in the room, and a voice call.

Cons

  • The preview needs wall generation in the client before a match exists. Today generation happens inside match creation.
  • Seat-on-board wants a board large enough to hold four labels. On a phone the corners collide and it falls back to a list.
  • Presets hide that boards can be 5 × 5 to 24 × 13, which is where the interesting questions are.
  • A real QR code is a dependency. The placeholder here is decoration.

What it answers

Not a UI requirement from the design doc, which is about reading the match, not starting one. It feeds two open questions: should the first boards be corridors, small grids, or both becomes answerable by rerolling and looking, and is the 5 × 5 minimum worth keeping becomes visible, because a small preview shows the wall percentage saturating. It also makes should walls be mirrored a thing you can see rather than reason about.

Leaves open: nothing about in-match reading. Ready state assumes everyone is present at once, which peer sessions do not guarantee; late joiners still need a rule.

Cost

M. Mode cards, presets, the fold-out, the share sheet, and the countdown are ui.tsx plus style.css. Seat-on-board reuses the existing board component with bodies hidden. The one thing that is not free is the preview: it needs the engine's wall generator callable from ConfigInput without constructing a Match, so the setup form can draw a board it has not created. That is a small export, not a rules change. A real QR code needs a generator, roughly 3 kB, or drop the tile.
